## Open to all forms of learning

Vrindavan is open to both day visits and overnight stays. The doors are open to anything that has a positive, enriching effect on people or society — the format and the subject are deliberately wide.

<Callout title="Our philosophy" variant="info">
  The possibilities are open-ended, as long as the activity supports focus, mindfulness, creativity, or social good.
</Callout>

## What can happen here

**Workshops, retreats, and conferences.** Vrindavan is well-suited to hands-on workshops in almost any subject — traditional crafts like pottery, weaving, and natural dyeing; modern subjects like coding, data science, and design; agriculture and permaculture; wellness practices like yoga and meditation; the performing arts. Anyone — partner or outside facilitator — can conduct a workshop here, provided the subject and approach fit the spirit of the place.

**Team offsites and deep-work days.** Startups and small teams come to Vrindavan for the kind of thinking that is hard to do in a city office. The campus is built for attention rather than entertainment, and that turns out to be exactly right for strategy days, planning sessions, brainstorming, and decisions that need quiet. Companies that have used the campus tend to come back for a different reason than they came the first time.

**Rejuvenation with family or friends.** Unlike a resort — where there is rarely anything real to do with parents or grandparents — Vrindavan is built for time spent together across generations. Walks in the gardens, meals together, watching children play freely in open ground, time for the kind of conversations that don't happen at home.

**Pilgrimage and personal reflection.** With the swayambhu Anantha Padmanabhaswamy and Bugga Ramalingeshwara Swamy temples close by, and the source of the Muchukunda nearby, the place is also used for short pilgrimage stays — a base from which to walk, sit, and visit the temples without the rush of a day trip.

## How a workshop works

Anyone — whether a Vrindavan partner or an outside facilitator — can conduct a workshop or programme that fits the spirit of the place. The criteria are simple: genuine expertise in the subject, respect for the values of the campus, and an ability to engage participants meaningfully.

The financial model is straightforward. A small participant fee covers facility maintenance, and the remaining amount goes to the facilitator as compensation. The structure is transparent, kept accessible to participants, and designed to keep the place sustainable rather than profitable.

The planning process starts with a short proposal — a description of the workshop, its objectives, duration, and what's needed from the space. After a brief conversation about logistics, Vrindavan provides the facilities while the facilitator takes care of the content. A short feedback loop after the workshop helps with future planning.

## Sample itineraries

For visitors who want a sense of how a day or two at Vrindavan might unfold, here are three rough plans. They are illustrative — the actual pace and order can be adjusted to taste — and they assume a winter visit, when the weather is most accommodating.

### One day

A one-day visit is enough to taste the place and visit the closest temple.

- **Morning:** Arrive at Vrindavan, settle in, and take a slow walk through the gardens and the property — the Mattillu, the amphitheatre, the zen and tropical gardens, the pond, and the walking track. Allow about ninety minutes.
- **Late morning:** Walk or drive to the **Anantha Padmanabhaswamy temple**, a few kilometres away. The temple is at its quietest before noon.
- **Lunch:** Return to Vrindavan for a community-style meal.
- **Afternoon:** A short forest walk on one of the nearby trekking trails, or simply rest under the trees. The trail that starts near the temple is a good option for a shorter walk.
- **Evening:** Tea on the terrace; watch the sun set over the hills before heading back.

### Two days

A second day allows for a temple, a forest walk, and an unhurried evening.

- **Day 1 morning:** Arrive, settle in, walk the property.
- **Day 1 late morning:** Visit the **Anantha Padmanabhaswamy temple**. Take time around the temple grounds and the surrounding forest.
- **Day 1 afternoon:** Lunch at Vrindavan, then a quiet afternoon — reading, conversation, or a session with the resident facilitator if one is available.
- **Day 1 evening:** Sunset walk, a meal, and stargazing from the terrace. The night sky here, away from city light, is one of the things people remember most.
- **Day 2 morning:** Early start for a forest trek. The longer of the two main trails (starting around half a kilometre from the temple, heading toward Kerelli) gives a fuller sense of the forest. Allow two to three hours.
- **Day 2 mid-day:** Return for lunch, rest, and a slower second half of the day before leaving.

### Three days

Three days lets you settle in, visit both major temples, and see the reservoir.

- **Day 1:** Arrive, walk the property, lunch, and an afternoon visit to the **Anantha Padmanabhaswamy temple**. Evening on the terrace.
- **Day 2 morning:** Forest trek (around two to three hours). Choose either of the trails near the temple, or one of the routes deeper into the Anantagiri forest.
- **Day 2 afternoon:** Visit the **Bugga Ramalingeshwara Swamy temple** — an ancient Shiva temple with a natural spring inside its complex. Drive or walk depending on energy and preference. Return for an evening meal at Vrindavan.
- **Day 3 morning:** Drive to **Kotepally Reservoir** (about twenty kilometres away). The reservoir is a popular weekend spot — large body of water in a scenic setting, with boating available. A picnic morning or a half-day outing.
- **Day 3 afternoon:** Return for lunch, a final walk through the gardens, and a slow departure.

<Callout title="Notes on these plans" variant="info">
  These itineraries are guidelines, not schedules. Temple timings, trek difficulty, and the time of year all influence the right pace. Most visitors find that two nights is the comfortable minimum to absorb the place; three or more start to feel different from a trip and more like a stay.
</Callout>

## Visitor experience

Mornings tend to start with birdsong. Workshops happen through the morning and early afternoon — focused, without distraction — and the evenings open up for soft sunsets, cultural performances, group discussions, or stargazing from the terrace.

Accommodation is simple and clean. The food is locally sourced and seasonal — usually vegetarian, prepared and eaten together. Shared spaces invite conversation. Children tend to do very well here, with plenty of open ground and the chance to discover plants, insects, and wildlife on their own.
