## Open to all forms of learning

Vrindavan is open to both day visits and overnight workshops. The doors are open to anything that has a positive, enriching effect on people or society — the format and the subject are deliberately wide.

<Callout title="Our philosophy" variant="info">
  The possibilities are open-ended, as long as the activity supports focus, mindfulness, creativity, or social good.
</Callout>

## What can happen here

**Arts and crafts.** Traditional crafts have a natural home at Vrindavan — pottery, handloom weaving, natural dyeing, woodworking, metalwork. Contemporary forms are equally welcome: painting, photography, digital art, creative writing, storytelling.

**Agriculture and sustainability.** Workshops on organic cultivation, permaculture, seed saving, and composting fit the place well. So do sessions on renewable energy, water conservation, waste reduction, and eco-friendly construction — much of which is already in practice on site.

**Wellness and contemplative practice.** Meditation, yoga, tai chi, and nature therapy take advantage of the silence and the open land. The space also lends itself to traditional knowledge — ayurveda, Sanskrit, Vedic studies, festival traditions.

**Technology and science.** The mud house and amphitheatre are equally suited to coding workshops, data science sessions, and discussions on machine learning or web development. Astronomy, botany, environmental science, and physics also fit naturally — the night sky alone makes a good classroom.

**Professional and team work.** Companies bring teams here for brainstorming, strategic planning, project work, and leadership development. The setting tends to produce more honest conversations and better focus than a hotel boardroom.

**Performing arts.** Music — classical, folk, contemporary — works well in the amphitheatre, as do classical and folk dance, and theatre. Sound healing, voice training, and movement work also have a place.

## How it works

Anyone — whether a Vrindavan partner or an outside facilitator — can conduct a workshop or programme that fits the spirit of the place. The criteria are simple: genuine expertise in the subject, respect for the values of the campus, and an ability to engage participants meaningfully. Past facilitators have included partners with diverse backgrounds, external subject experts, teachers, artists, craftspeople, and community members with knowledge worth sharing.

The financial model is straightforward. A small participant fee covers facility maintenance, and the remaining amount goes to the facilitator as compensation. The structure is transparent, kept accessible to participants, and designed to keep the place sustainable rather than profitable.

The planning process starts with a short proposal — a description of the workshop, its objectives, duration, and what's needed from the space. After a brief conversation about logistics, Vrindavan provides the facilities while the facilitator takes care of the content. A short feedback loop after the workshop helps with future planning.

## What a visit feels like

A typical day starts with birdsong. Workshops happen through the morning and early afternoon — focused, without distraction — and the evenings open up for soft sunsets, cultural performances, group discussions, or stargazing from the terrace.

Accommodation is simple and clean, in bedrooms with natural materials and quiet enough for restful sleep. Shared spaces invite conversation; meals are usually prepared and eaten together. The food is locally sourced and seasonal — simple, nutritious, and prepared with the same mindfulness the rest of the campus runs on.

Children tend to do very well here. With no screens and plenty of open space, they discover plants, insects, and wildlife on their own, and fall back into traditional play with natural materials. Plant identification, wildlife observation, and small crafts give them more to do than they'll get to in a single visit.

For adults, the absence of distraction usually does more than any structured programme. Concentration deepens, conversations slow down, and the time tends to be remembered for what wasn't there as much as for what was.

## Where things happen

Indoors, the **main hall** hosts most large workshops, professional meetings, meditation sessions, and performances. The **kitchen** doubles as a space for cooking workshops, nutrition sessions, and community meal preparation. The **bedrooms** work for small group discussions, mentoring conversations, and quiet study.

Outdoors, the **amphitheatre** holds performances, presentations, storytelling, and celebrations. The **zen garden** is the place for walking meditation, nature observation, and quiet contemplation. The **open lawns** support physical activities, group games, and outdoor workshops, and the **botanical garden** itself is a working classroom for plant identification, herbal studies, and ecology.

## Through the year

The **monsoon** (June to September) brings lush green landscapes and cooler temperatures. Most outdoor activity is still possible, but heavier rains push some sessions indoors. The **winter** (October to February) is the peak season — perfect weather, clear skies for stargazing, and comfortable temperatures around the clock. The **summer** (March to May) shifts the rhythm: mornings and evenings are the active hours, mature trees provide shade through the day, and the orchards are in fruit.
