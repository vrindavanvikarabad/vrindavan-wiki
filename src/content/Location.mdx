## Around Anantagiri Hills

Vrindavan sits at the foothills of Anantagiri, one of the more beautiful forested regions in Telangana. The hills are also the source of the Muchukunda — the river later called the Musi, which eventually joins the Krishna. Within a short distance of the campus there are ancient temples, dense forest, viewpoints, a large reservoir, and small towns worth visiting.

## Getting there

Vrindavan is about **70 kilometres west of Hyderabad**, an hour and a half to two hours by road depending on traffic. The nearest sizeable town is Vikarabad, the district headquarters, which serves as the practical anchor for the area — shops, ATMs, hospitals, and a railway junction.

**By road.** Most visitors drive or are driven from Hyderabad. The route is straightforward and signposted; the last few kilometres into the foothills are gentle and scenic. A private cab arranged in advance is by far the most reliable option — both for the journey out and the return — since ride-share availability in the Anantagiri area is unpredictable and patchy. We recommend arranging a cab through your hotel or a Hyderabad-based service for the whole trip.

**By train.** Vikarabad Junction is the nearest railway station, connected to Secunderabad and on the route toward Wadi. Trains run regularly. From the station, a short local taxi or auto ride brings you to Anantagiri.

**By bus.** Telangana state buses run between Hyderabad and Vikarabad. Onward connections from Vikarabad to Anantagiri are available but infrequent — again, a short local taxi or pre-arranged ride is easier.

## Food and meals

Most of the eating at Vrindavan happens on the campus itself, where the food is locally sourced, seasonal, and usually South Indian vegetarian — the simple Udupi-style meals familiar across much of South India. Cooking is often a shared activity during workshops.

For meals outside the campus, **Vikarabad town** is the practical option, with a range of small restaurants offering South Indian, Andhra-style, and basic North Indian food. There are no fine-dining options nearby and very little in the way of large-chain restaurants — the food culture is local, modest, and mostly vegetarian. We can suggest a few specific places to visitors on request; the landscape changes often enough that printed recommendations age badly.

### Anantha Padmanabhaswamy Temple

About five kilometres from Vrindavan, this ancient *swayambhu* temple is dedicated to Lord Anantha Padmanabhaswamy, a form of Vishnu — and gives the Anantagiri hills their name. It is a popular pilgrimage destination, known for its architecture, its setting in the hills, and the panoramic views from around the temple. Early morning and evening tend to be the quietest times to visit; festivals and full moon days draw larger crowds. Basic amenities and parking are available, and a Telangana government-operated hotel sits near the temple.

### Bugga Ramalingeshwara Swamy Temple

A short drive from Vrindavan, this is an ancient Shiva temple set in a quiet forest. Its main draw is a natural spring inside the temple complex — a freshwater source forming a small natural pool, considered sacred by devotees and used for ritual bathing. The temple is open through the day and well connected by road.

## Natural attractions

### The Anantagiri forest

The Anantagiri forest is dense, deciduous, and rich in wildlife. Two main trekking trails start near the temple — one from the temple itself, and another about half a kilometre away, heading toward Kerelli. The trails are well-known to local trekkers but uncrowded on most days. The forest also has teak (Tectona grandis) among its older species, and the air is noticeably cooler than the plains. Birdwatching is best in the early morning. The forest is therapeutic in a way that resists explanation.

### Viewpoints

Several viewpoints are scattered across the hills, three to ten kilometres from Vrindavan. A few require short hikes; others are accessible by road. The best are at sunrise and sunset — overlooks across green valleys, treetop panoramas, and wider views from the higher points. Most have basic paths and a few seating areas.

### The Muchukunda's source

The Muchukunda begins near Vrindavan and flows through the hills, eventually joining the Krishna as the Musi. It is a small mountain stream — fresh, seasonal, and varying significantly in flow through the year — but it gives the area its sacred geography. According to local tradition, the river takes its name from the sage-king Muchukunda. Along its course are natural pools, rock formations, and a fair amount of wildlife.

### Kotepally Reservoir

About twenty kilometres from Anantagiri, the Kotepally reservoir (also known as Nagasamudram Lake) is a large body of water in a scenic setting, with a well-maintained earthen dam. It has become a popular weekend destination — boating and kayaking are available, and there are picnic spots around the water. On weekends the place can get quite busy.

## Vikarabad and nearby villages

Vikarabad town (the district headquarters) was once the seat of a princely state, and still carries traces of that history — colonial-era buildings, traditional markets, old palace ruins, and a mix of temples and mosques. It is also the practical town for the area, with shops, restaurants, ATMs, and hospitals.

The villages around Anantagiri offer a much closer look at rural Telangana life — traditional farming, local crafts, seasonal festivals, and warm communities. They are good places to slow down and watch a different rhythm of life unfold; many craftspeople are happy to talk about their work to anyone genuinely curious.

## Trails and rocks

The trekking around Anantagiri spans easy walks suitable for any age, moderate hikes of a few hours, and longer full-day routes. The most popular routes head to summit views, loop through dense woodland, follow streams, or connect rural villages. The hills also have interesting rock formations and small caves, occasionally used by experienced rock climbers.

## Practical notes

A few small reminders for visitors:

Temple visits ask for modest dress and a respectful attitude toward local photography rules. Carry enough water and let someone know your plans before any longer hike. Engage with local communities the way you would want a visitor to engage with yours. And for transport — arrange your cab in advance for the whole trip, both outward and return. Calling a ride from Anantagiri at short notice can be slow.
